# Does Being in Revit Win Specs? Evidence Memo
**For:** AI Council · **From:** Chris, Director of AI & Technology · **Date:** Sep 27, 2026 · **Companion to:** QCo-Revit-Bet-Exec-Brief.md

## Bottom line
- **The evidence is strong that specifiers expect BIM content. It is weak that BIM content causes spec wins.** Surveys show most designers use manufacturer BIM objects and want them. I found **no independent study** that links BIM objects or Revit plugins to win rate or revenue. The outcome claims all come from vendors, and they report downloads and leads, not wins.
- **BIM content is now table stakes, not a differentiator.** Most manufacturers already provide it. Missing content is a loss risk. Having it rarely sets you apart.
- **Lighting is a harder case than most building products.** Lighting designers' use of Revit varies. The luminaire schedule and spec control the job, not the model. Rep "packaging" and value engineering (VE) swap products at bid.
- **Our break-even (about 1 extra large project a year) is a low bar, but outside evidence can't show we'd clear it.** Only our own data can. Run the tests in the last section before we fund Gate 2.

## Counter-evidence
- **Substitution is normal, even by the vendor's own numbers** (35% often, 43% sometimes). In lighting, packaging and VE happen at bid, after the model is done (IALD).
- **Reps outrank BIM** in what US architects value (AIA 2023).
- **Table stakes:** 73% of suppliers already provide objects (NBS 2025). Presence defends; it rarely differentiates.
- **Selection bias:** the BIMobject figures come from people who already use BIMobject.
- **Placeholders and quality:** heavy or broken manufacturer families get swapped for generics. Even BIMsmith says bad content gets products "removed from the model" ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bimsmith_wearearchitects-bim-revit-activity-7460660226137133056-L6k4)). This source is weak.
- No study shows that downloads correlate with orders.

## Lighting specifics
- **Tools:** lighting designers use AGi32 and DIALux. ElumTools runs the AGi32 engine inside Revit using native Revit families ([LAI](https://lightinganalysts.com/elumtools/)). Revit fixture families carry IES files as "photometric web" data ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Revit-DocumentPresent/files/GUID-4F1C8175-0F14-4C45-AA08-CE0304A15700.htm)). **Accurate IES files per configuration matter more than the geometry.**
- **What competitors do:**
  - **Acuity** sells a Visual Lighting Plugin for Revit: auto-layout, calculations, schedules back to Revit, any maker's IES; license required after a 30-day trial; latest build 9/17/2026 ([Visual](https://www.visual-3d.com/software/downloadvisuallightingplugin.aspx)).
  - **Cooper** calls itself "the lighting industry's BIM leader," with native Revit files, a BIMobject presence and in-house BIM support ([Cooper](https://www.cooperlighting.com/global/revit-files)).
  - **Focal Point** has a BIM/Revit library, and **Axis** posts Revit files per product ([Axis](https://www.axislighting.com/products/stencilflex-creative-collection)).
  - **Coronet** has Revit files, but they are dated 2019 ([Coronet](https://coronetled.com/downloads/)).
  - **Signify** and **Lutron/Ketra** are on BIMsmith and BIMobject ([Signify](https://market.bimsmith.com/signify), [Ketra](https://www.bimobject.com/en/lutronelectronics/product/lutron-ketra-d4r)).
  - **Diode LED:** the homepage shows IES files but I found no Revit content. For Tivoli, Luminii and WAC, the homepages show only web configurators (not conclusive). Pure Lighting: not verified.
  - I did **not** identify which competitor has the BOM-from-model plugin. Sales should name it.

## Internal tests (30–60 days, low cost)
1. **Lost-deal review:** list large (≥$250k) losses from the last 24 months and interview ~10 reps about each. Was the competitor's Revit content or plugin a factor? *If fewer than 1 large loss a year traces to it, the case is weak.*
2. **Drawing and schedule audit:** have reps pull 20–30 recent bid sets and luminaire schedules. Count competitor family names and type marks, and note whether QCo was basis-of-design or an alternate.
3. **Top-10 lighting design firm calls:** Do you model in Revit, or does the MEP engineer? Do you use manufacturer or in-house families? Would per-configuration IES plus a QCo family change your choice? Has the competitor's plugin?
4. **CRM tagging:** add "spec source" and loss reasons ("Revit/BIM," "packaged/VE'd") to every opportunity for 60 days.
5. **Download pilot:** post 2–3 line-based rigid families with IES (~$1.5–4.5k at $500–1,500 each) on our site with light gating, then match downloading firms to CRM opportunities.
6. **Spec-erosion check:** of our spec wins, how many were lost at bid to packaging or VE? If that number is large, Revit won't fix it. Spec protection will.

**Decision rule:** proceed to Gate 2 if the tests show at least 1 large project a year lost where competitor model content was a stated factor, or if at least 3 of the top 10 firms say manufacturer Revit content changes their selection.
