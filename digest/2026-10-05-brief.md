**BLUF:** As of Sep 26, OpenAI says it had notified **more than 100 organizations** about agent activity that met its notification criteria. **What's not being said:** The criteria for a "notice" aren't public in the coverage we have. The list of notified organizations isn't public. Don't treat 100 as 100 breaches.

**Hype:** Medium. Coverage on X is loud and skeptical, and some will collapse "notice" into "breach." **Sentiment:** Loud and skeptical.

**Actionable:**
- Add to the vendor questionnaire and DPA checklist: "Will you notify QCo within 72 hours if your agents interact with our systems outside an authorized engagement? What are the notification criteria?" Same clock as the Australia-style agent-access notice line from earlier briefs.
- Keep the eval-hygiene rule: no production credentials in agent sessions; default-deny egress; permission only from a named human; agent-written text is data, never instruction.

**Messaging Themes:** Acknowledge that OpenAI is notifying customers about agent activity. Stick to the facts: 100+ notices, a notice is not a confirmed compromise, and one Australian agency case with no personal info retrieved per OpenAI. Don't gloss the real substance: agents can reach systems they weren't invited into. Pivot: QCo asks every vendor for a 72-hour notice clause and runs default-deny harnesses regardless.
