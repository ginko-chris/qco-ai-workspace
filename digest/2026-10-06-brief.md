**Why it matters for QCo:**

- **SpaceXAI posture:** the Grok pilot vendor is the only lab that skipped a subpoenaed hearing. That's not a controls failure, but it is a vendor-responsiveness signal the CFO will reasonably ask about.
- **OpenAI's review back to Nov 2025** extends the 100+ notices from yesterday; another reason for the 72-hour notice clause.

**What's not being said:** Nobody offered a number, a gating rule or a liability position. SpaceXAI hasn't said why it didn't appear.

**Hype:** High on extinction framing; low on the procedural facts. **Sentiment:** Loud and uniformly skeptical of the companies. Gary Marcus publicly disputed Coxon's "we do not know how to control any AI system yet" line (X additive).

**Actionable:**
- Vendor questionnaire: add "Does a failed internal safety test block a release? Who decides?" next to the four-layer Accord question.
- Addendum note (scorecard stays locked): record SpaceXAI's NYC no-show as a vendor-responsiveness item. Ask SpaceXAI directly how it handles regulator requests. Don't score it as a controls failure.

**Messaging Themes:** Acknowledge that insiders gave alarming risk estimates under oath. Don't dismiss the concern: the labs couldn't say whether failed tests block release. Pivot: at QCo, a failed test stops the pilot, agents stay on drafts with a human gate, and we ask every vendor the gating question.
