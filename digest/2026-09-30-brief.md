### 1. OpenAI "dots": always-on agents with their own computers, now in Slack and Teams — **Score 4/5**
**Sources:** OpenAI "Introducing dots" and DevDay recap (primary), TechCrunch, Verge, Sep 29

**BLUF:** Dots are persistent GPT-6 Astra agents. Each one has its own cloud computer and browser, connects to more than 4,000 apps, and can be reached in ChatGPT, Slack, Teams and by voice. The controls are close to our own list:
- per-action allow, require-approval, or block rules
- auto-review of any action that affects an account
- an activity view of what the dot is doing
- the ability to pause a dot
- saved passwords the model never sees
- background research that's read-only

Dots are rolling out to Pro and **Business Premium** now. Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare get them only if an admin turns them on. "Specialist dots," in preview, are owned by the organization, with their own identity and credentials. They're governed through Microsoft Agent 365, and OpenAI's own test areas are procurement, invoicing, support and contracting. The live demo stalled.

**Why it matters for QCo:** This is the third persistent-agent product in a week, after Copilot Autopilot and Grok Team Bots, and it lands in the tools employees already use. On a Business Premium plan it can switch on without a Council decision. The specialist dots' test areas (invoicing, procurement) sit right next to NetSuite, where no agent gets standing write access.

**What's not being said:** There's no audit-log export detail, no data-residency statement for dot computers, and no word on whether an admin can block a dot's plugins by category. The approval rules are set by the user, not enforced by an admin, below Enterprise.

**Hype:** Medium-high. The scope is real, but the demo failed live and it arrives a week after two rogue-agent incidents. **Sentiment:** Very loud and split. People are bullish on scope and skeptical on reliability. Two open-source clones appeared within an hour.

**Actionable:**
- One question for the MSP or admin: which ChatGPT plan, if any, does QCo have? If it's Business Premium or Enterprise, keep dots **off** until the Council decides. Also confirm who can connect plugins.
- Add dots as a fourth column in the third-option comparison, as an addendum only because the scorecard is locked. It compares directly with Team Bots on the same criteria: admin-enforced approval gates, credential isolation, audit trail, and staying stopped once blocked.
- Specialist dots touching procurement or invoicing are out of scope while "no standing NetSuite write" holds.

### 2. Anthropic Red Team: China's open-weight GLM-5.3 builds working browser exploits cheaply — **Score 4/5**
**Sources:** Anthropic research (primary), NIST CAISI assessment, Sep 29

**BLUF:** GLM-5.3 comes close to Anthropic's unreleased Mythos Preview on end-to-end exploit building (50 of 410 against 56 of 410). A GLM-5.3-Flash run built a working Chrome exploit chain for an already-known vulnerability for **about $20 in API cost**. Its safeguards come off easily: a deceptive prompt got 64% compliance, pre-filling its reasoning got 92%, and removing the safety tuning got 100%. NIST CAISI calls it the most cyber-capable open-weight model, about four months behind the US frontier.

**Why it matters for QCo:** Our risk here isn't China policy. It's the cost of turning a published browser bug into a working attack, which is now about $20 for anyone. The time between a patch being released and our machines getting it is where that attack works. That's weather QCo controls through the MSP.

**What's not being said:** This is Anthropic testing a competitor while asking for "broader defender access" to frontier models, so there's a commercial angle. Still, NIST's separate assessment says much the same.

**Hype:** Low-medium. The method is published and a government assessment backs it. **Sentiment:** Loud in security circles and moderate elsewhere, with export-control politics attached.

**Actionable:** Ask the MSP two things. What's the enforced browser auto-update and restart window on QCo endpoints, and what's the patch SLA for critical browser and OS fixes? A target of 72 hours or less for criticals is reasonable. This fits on the same MSP call as the key sweep and the Copilot tenant question.

**Messaging Themes:** Acknowledge that a Chinese open-weight model can now build real exploits cheaply. State the numbers and NIST's view without China alarmism. Don't gloss it: the cost of attacks just fell. Pivot to QCo: we patch browsers fast, we don't run open-weight models in the agent path, and the MSP is on a patch clock.

### 5. White House "Accord on Super Intelligence": voluntary four-layer controls; "SI" rename order — **Score 3/5**
**Sources:** Washington Examiner (accord text), CNBC, Reuters/CNA, Fox Business, Sep 29

**BLUF:** Pichai, Zuckerberg, Amodei, **Musk**, Huang and Brockman signed a voluntary accord that Trump called "morally binding." Each lab commits to four layers: internal controls on capability and alignment (cyber, bio, chem, and no unintended system access), an internal team that verifies those controls, an independent external auditor, and a board committee overseeing the results. An executive order tells federal agencies to say "Super Intelligence" instead of "AI." A 10-person safety board is "being considered."

**Why:** No rule changes for QCo. But the four layers give QCo a free, bipartisan-sounding vendor question: "Have you implemented the accord's four layers, and will you share your external auditor's summary?" It costs nothing to ask, and it separates vendors that have done the work from those that only signed.

**What's not being said:** There's no enforcement, no named auditor, and no timeline. The rename is branding, not policy.

**Hype:** High. **Sentiment:** Very loud. Policy press is skeptical and calls it thin and voluntary; the right is bullish. A Reuters/Ipsos poll has 73% worried the labs haven't done enough.

**Actionable:** Add the four-layer question to the AI vendor questionnaire and DPA checklist, next to the 72-hour notice clause and the developer-indemnity line.

**Messaging Themes:** Acknowledge that the labs made a public safety pledge at the White House. State what it says (four layers, voluntary) and skip the "SI" rename, which is framing, not substance. Don't gloss it: voluntary means unenforced. Pivot to QCo: we ask every vendor to show those controls, and we run our own regardless.
